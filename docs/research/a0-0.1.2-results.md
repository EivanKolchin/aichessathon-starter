# A0 0.1.2 — search speed, clock reliability and supplied-game audit

10 September 2026. This is a development checkpoint, not evidence of Stockfish-level strength.
The root agent and the frozen `a0-012` opponent now use 0.1.2. The 0.1.0 reference remains available.

## What changed

Quiet promotions in quiescence now generate moves only from pawns on their penultimate rank
to empty destinations. Capture order, underpromotions and check evasions are preserved.
The first version of this change was 0.1.1.

Longer games exposed a clock defect inherited from 0.1.0. On this Windows/Python 3.12 host,
`monotonic()` uses GetTickCount64 with a reported 15.625 ms resolution. An internal deadline
could arrive too late to return a move within the referee's clock. The referee remains unchanged.
Version 0.1.2 uses `perf_counter()` (reported resolution 0.0001 ms), checks every search node for
deadlines below 250 ms, and reserves 30–100 ms for preparation and protocol overhead. This is
local reliability evidence; it does not certify behaviour under arbitrary OS stalls or on the
organiser's hardware.

The lab also now counts a candidate's clock failures when the referee awards a draw, and counts
failures in void games where both agents failed. Previously only failure losses were counted.
Historical summaries are recalculated on read, preserving the recorded manifests and PGNs.

## Controlled measurements

| Experiment | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-node comparison against 0.1.0 | 116/116 identical search results; 1.118× geometric mean speed ratio | About 11.8% more throughput, or 10.6% less time for the same work, on this host. |
| 20 games against frozen 0.1.0 | 14 wins, 3 draws, 3 losses; no candidate failures | All 14 wins were checkmates. One draw was the old reference flagging against our bare king. |
| 18 games against positional, tactical and rollout diagnostics | 18 wins; no candidate failures | Useful correctness screen; these are weak opponents. |
| 6 games against Stockfish 19 | 6 losses, all checkmates | No demonstrated progress against Stockfish. |
| 8 follow-up games from supplied timeout openings | 8 wins, all checkmates; no candidate failures | Reproduced the relevant opening/opponent/clock conditions after the clock fix. |

There were **52 completed 0.1.2 games with no candidate failures** across these three batches.
The initial 44 used 5s + 50ms; the supplied-data follow-up used 10s + 100ms. All used the full
600-ply cap, paired colours, one game at a time and frozen source copies. These short local
clocks are research settings. Final records and legal continuations were replay-verified.

The 20-game head-to-head score was 77.5%, but its exploratory pair-level Hoeffding interval is
34.6–100%, including 50%. Do not convert it into a claimed Elo gain. Speed, different clock
allocation, host variability and a clock-related draw are mixed in this game result.

The microbenchmark used 20 development positions and nine edge/history fixtures, four repeats,
5,000-node limits, fresh processes/caches and an untimed warm-up. Build order alternated and
fixture order was shuffled. Moves, scores, depth, node counts, quiescence counts, transposition
hits, stopping status and PVs matched exactly. The timing aggregate excludes the nine special
fixtures and combines per-position median ratios. Eighteen of 20 timing positions were faster;
this is descriptive timing, without a confidence interval. The earlier 0.1.1-only benchmark
measured 1.076×; host variation prevents attributing the difference to the clock change alone.

Twenty direct 0.1.2 calls on those exact final positions, with their recorded remaining clocks
and a 95 ms estimated increment, all returned legal moves within the local referee clock.
The smallest measured margin was approximately 30 ms. These calls used fresh history and
excluded IPC, so they were supplemented with full games. The follow-up matched the original
Solid build hash, FENs, pair seeds and time control across all four openings and both colours:
the original subset scored 4/2/2 with four failures; 0.1.2 scored 8/0/0 with none. Eight games
are a regression check, not a general reliability guarantee or an unseen strength test.

The six Stockfish losses were all checkmates from Bird, Reti and Queen's Gambit Accepted
positions. In the 169 retained candidate telemetry rows from those games, 165 searches finished
at depth 2 or 3, and 93.5% of recorded nodes were quiescence nodes. Logs retain only their
beginning and end, so this is a diagnostic sample, not an unbiased estimate over every move.
It supports prioritising search cost and tactical reach; it does not isolate the cause of
each loss or measure move quality against an independent evaluator.

## Next experiment

Use 0.1.2 as the reliable development reference. First profile the quiescence work represented
in the supplied loss positions and separate time spent generating moves, checking draws,
ordering captures and evaluating positions. Preserve exact-node equivalence for changes that
only reduce overhead. Changes that prune search need separate tactical tests, including checks,
promotions, sacrifices and zugzwang, followed by paired full games.

The larger research priority is an original compiled search/move-generation backend, with
python-chess as its legality oracle, before broad evolutionary tuning of architecture or a
trained evaluator. Keep overhead optimisation, selective search and mating conversion as
separate proposals so their effects can be identified. Do not train or tune on an opening set
and then report it as unseen validation. Neither an agentic move loop nor genetic parameter
search has yet shown evidence of addressing the depth deficit observed here.
